The program

Mar 5–10

Oil Painting Basics

One object at a time

You'll start by painting a single object on white cloth, then a single object on patterned fabric. This is where you learn to see values in color: how light and dark work once color comes in.

Objects together

Then you'll paint several objects of different materials together, and end with a group of objects in a real space.

What you'll leave with

  • Your own oil paintings of still lifes, painted from real objects.
  • How to see values in color, and use them as you paint.
  • How to paint different textures, such as porcelain, fabric and metal.
  • A way of practicing that you can keep up at home.

Academic approach

The classes follow an academic method, which means you start simple and build up one step at a time.

  • Your teachers are academically trained Vietnamese artists, and they teach in English.
  • The course materials are inspired by the official training of the Vietnam University of Fine Arts.
  • You'll paint from real objects set up in front of you, not from photos.
  • Oil paint dries slowly, so there's one class a day, and your painting rests until the next one.

Good to know

Before you apply, see if this is the right fit for you

We stay in Tây Hồ, by West Lake

Photos of West Lake: Nguyen Ngoc Tien and Linh Tran, on Pexels.

  • Tây Hồ is the district around West Lake. It's a diverse area, with cafés and restaurants of many cuisines. Lotte Mall is nearby for groceries and anything you forgot to pack.
  • Your evenings are free. When you want the busy side of Hanoi, the Old Quarter is a short taxi ride away.
  • The apartments and the studio are both down lanes that cars can't enter, which is normal in Hanoi, and it's how you get to see the everyday city up close. Every ride starts and ends with a short walk, so you'll want to be comfortable walking on uneven streets.

You have your own serviced apartment

  • You stay in your own serviced apartment in Tây Hồ, Hanoi's West Lake district, near Lotte Mall. It's yours alone, with a double bed, a private bathroom and your own kitchen. It's an aparthotel, not a hotel: it's cleaned once a week, in the middle of the week. The building has a lift.
  • We all stay in the same building, so the group is always close by, and you still have a quiet place of your own at the end of the day.
  • You're welcome to bring a companion at no extra charge. Your companion isn't part of the program: the classes, the welcome dinner and our shared car to the studio are for participants only.
  • Two people joining together can share one apartment, with one double bed. A shared apartment can't also take a companion. See Sharing an apartment, under Fee.

Are meals included?

  • Only our welcome buffet dinner on the first evening is included. Your apartment has a kitchen, and Lotte Mall is nearby if you'd like to buy groceries.
  • Lunch is on your own. Class ends at noon, so you're free to eat wherever you like, near the apartments or anywhere in the city.

Are flights included?

  • Flights are not included. That leaves you free to plan the trip your way. Come a few days early or stay on afterwards to see more of Vietnam.

Do I need a visa?

  • It depends on your passport. Some passports can enter Vietnam without a visa for a set number of days.
  • Rules change and differ by country, so please check the current rule for your passport before you book anything. If you plan to arrive early or stay on, make sure your whole stay fits within what your passport allows.

What's the weather like?

  • March in Hanoi is cool and mild, usually around 18 to 23°C. Skies are often gray, with light drizzle on many days. It's comfortable weather for walking around the city in your free afternoons.

What should I bring?

  • A light jacket or a few layers, and a small umbrella for the drizzle. Bring comfortable shoes for walking too.

How do we get to the art studio?

  • Transport to and from the art studio is included. We share one car there and back, to keep the week affordable.

What about laundry?

  • The building has a washer and dryer.

Do I need to know how to paint?

  • No, you don't need to have painted in oil before. Drawing experience is nice to have. The classes teach oil painting, not drawing.
