[ The program ]

The one-week program focuses on Pencil. The second week adds Composition and Sketching.

Week one: Pencil

Light, shadow and form

You'll start with cubes, spheres and the space around them. This is where you learn to shade with light and shadow, measure what you see, and place a drawing well on the page.

The head and a still life

You'll learn to draw the head by breaking it into simple flat planes, then draw a classical bust. The week ends with a still life full of different textures.

What you'll leave with

  • Your own pencil drawings: basic forms, head studies and a finished still life.
  • The basics of academic drawing: measuring what you see, and shading with light and shadow.
  • A way of practicing that you can keep up at home.

Week two: Composition and Sketching

Left: two pages on composition from Drawing Made Easy by E. G. Lutz, 1921.

Composition

You'll study how Old Master paintings are put together, then design compositions of your own and keep improving them.

Sketching

You'll learn to draw quickly from what is in front of you: everyday objects first, then different ways to draw texture, then a whole corner of a room. Most of it is in the studio; a few sessions are outdoors, sketching the studio's building.

What you'll leave with

  • Everything from week one.
  • Compositions you designed yourself.
  • Sketches drawn from life, in the studio and outside it.

Academic approach

The classes follow an academic method, which means you start with simple forms and build up one step at a time.

  • Your teachers are academically trained Vietnamese artists, and they teach in English.
  • The course materials are inspired by the official training of the Vietnam University of Fine Arts.
  • You'll draw from real objects set up in front of you, not from photos.

[ Good to know ]

Before you apply, here are some things that you should know to see if this is the right fit for you.

We stay in Tây Hồ, by West Lake

Photos of West Lake: Nguyen Ngoc Tien and Linh Tran, on Pexels.

  • Tây Hồ is the district around West Lake. It's a diverse area, with cafés and restaurants of many cuisines. Lotte Mall is nearby for groceries and anything you forgot to pack.
  • Your evenings are free. When you want the busy side of Hanoi, the Old Quarter is a short taxi ride away.
  • The apartments and the studio are both down lanes that cars can't enter, which is normal in Hanoi, and it's how you get to see the everyday city up close. Every ride starts and ends with a short walk, so you'll want to be comfortable walking on uneven streets.

You have your own serviced apartment

  • You stay in your own serviced apartment in Tây Hồ, Hanoi's West Lake district, near Lotte Mall. It's yours alone, with a double bed, a private bathroom and your own kitchen. It's an aparthotel, not a hotel: it's cleaned once a week, in the middle of the week. The building has a lift.
  • We all stay in the same building, so the group is always close by, and you still have a quiet place of your own at the end of the day.
  • You're welcome to bring a companion at no extra charge. Your companion isn't part of the intensive: the classes, the welcome dinner and our shared car to the studio are for participants only.
  • Two people joining the intensive together can share one apartment, with one double bed. A shared apartment can't also take a companion. See Sharing an apartment, under How to apply.

Meals are your own

  • Only our welcome buffet dinner on the first evening is included. Your apartment has a kitchen, and Lotte Mall is nearby if you'd like to buy groceries.
  • Lunch is on your own. There's no group lunch, so the two-hour break is yours to eat wherever you like. A few places are a short walk from the studio, or you can take a taxi further, at your own cost.

Flights are not included

  • That leaves you free to plan the trip your way. Come a few days early or stay on afterwards to see more of Vietnam.

Do I need a visa?

  • It depends on your passport. Some passports can enter Vietnam without a visa for a set number of days.
  • Rules change and differ by country, so please check the current rule for your passport before you book anything. If you plan to arrive early or stay on, make sure your whole stay fits within what your passport allows.

What's the weather like?

  • March in Hanoi is cool and mild, usually around 18 to 23°C. Skies are often gray, with light drizzle on many days. It's comfortable weather for walking around and for long days in the art studio.

What should I bring?

  • A light jacket or a few layers, and a small umbrella for the drizzle. Bring comfortable shoes for walking too.

How do we get to the art studio?

  • Transport to and from the art studio is included. We share one car there and back, to keep the intensive affordable.

What about laundry?

  • The building has a washer and dryer.
